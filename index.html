Core curriculum

The six LARK modules

A kit that can deepen students' interest in and exposure to the world of language endangerment. No prior experience required.

🎙️
Start here — Module 1
Elicitation Protocol
Real languages live not in thought-up phrases but in stories, arguments, instructions, narratives. This module shifts interactions from interrogation to conversation.

Module 1

Elicitation Protocol

8 lessons · 4 activities

Students use a set of visual prompt cards (20–100). Prompters are images selected to provoke narratives across mutual or universal themes. Students write a shadow transcript noting non-verbal cues, gestures, and tone shifts.

Start
🗺️
Module 2

Language Mapping

6 lessons · 3 activities

Students take a map of their neighborhood, city, or region and mark the location of a person in the vicinity and the corresponding original location of their native language. Then consult the simplified UNESCO Language Vitality and Endangerment framework.

Start
🤝
Module 3

Apprentice-Partner Model

10 lessons · 5 activities

Students enter a structured partnership of 2–8 sessions of 45–90 minutes each. The defining rule: no translation. Activities include walking, gardening, cooking, making art, playing games, and telling stories. Students keep an apprentice journal.

Start
📰
Module 4

Magazine Portrait

7 lessons · 4 activities

Students write a one-to-two page language portrait: the language's place of origin, phonetic details, a speaker biography, and story excerpts. After completing it, students distribute at least 15 copies to friends, family, neighbors, libraries, and cafes.

Start
📊
Module 5

Language Vitality Assessment

9 lessons · 3 activities

Students set up 1–2 sessions to go through questions on the Vitality card. They give a score of 1 through 5 for each category, then tally the score out of 45. This gives an estimation of the language's state of vitality.

Start
🌱
Module 6

Digital Seed Archive

5 lessons · 2 activities

An online interface where students submit files, documents, and recordings from their learning experience. The team at Lost Worlds Institute reviews each submission and, after organization and consideration, may publish the created resources.

Start
Why it matters

Preserving endangered languages

Preserving endangered languages is a community-led effort. It would not be possible without engagement with other people, organizations, and communities.

"40% of the world's roughly 7,000 languages are at risk of disappearing within the next three generations. The next speakers, those who will carry on the traditions of these languages, are of youth age."

🌍

Youth are first responders

With language shift rising and social pressures to move away from Indigenous tongues, structured methodologies are needed. LARK is a youth-led framework that can guide students interested in doing this work.

📖

From interrogation to conversation

Traditional language documentation asks speakers to translate sentences or name objects. This produces thin, artificial data. Real languages live not in thought-up phrases but in stories, arguments, instructions, narratives.

🏫

Open access curriculum

This kit may be distributed and repurposed however schools, institutions, or individuals see fit, with credit to the original source. Not just PDFs and resources but an open-access service for K-12 students.

🔬

AP Seminar and research

LARK connects to AP Seminar and AP Research methodologies. Students build portfolios of real fieldwork they can present to schools, competitions, and organizations like NACLO.

🤲

Community and consent

It is important to respect communities in all manners of engagement. This kit is not a replacement of traditional language preservation methods but a framework to guide students interested in this work.

📡

Youth-led attracts youth

Lost Worlds Institute operates on the principle that youth leading the charge attracts more youth. Students can bring LARK to their own schools and administrations, almost like chapters of a club.

Module 2

Language Mapping

Students take a map of their neighborhood, city, or region. They mark the location of a given person and the corresponding original location of their native language. Ideally, students push beyond their family to engage their community.

Map the languages around you

Once mapped, students consult LARK's simplified version of UNESCO's Language Vitality and Endangerment framework to understand each language's status within its society and community.

Progress system

Three levels of mastery

Each module has its own mastery levels. Complete lessons, finish fieldwork activities, and earn recognition from Lost Worlds Institute.

1

Learner

Core lessons complete. Ready to start your first elicitation session with a speaker in your community.

2

Documentarian

Fieldwork activities complete. Shadow transcript produced and at least one heritage language speaker mapped.

3

Field Linguist

All 6 modules complete. Work uploaded to the Digital Seed Archive and reviewed by Lost Worlds Institute.
